# Museum guide mascot recommendation

## What the current mascot already gets right

The existing **restless idea** has three strong identity cues worth protecting:

- one oversized eye, which makes attention visible immediately;
- orbiting geometry, which connects it to Akshit's moving GitHub waveform;
- a deliberately imperfect inked silhouette, which belongs in the handmade museum.

Its weak point is emotional readability. A tall dark body, tiny legs, and a single unblinking eye can read as a watcher in the corner. The replacement should not lose the oddness; it should add an unmistakable invitation.

## Comparison

| Study | First impression | Motion signature | Museum fit | Identity distinctiveness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Orbit kitten | immediately cute and familiar | breathing body, tracking tail | friendly host | medium; the cat category is crowded |
| Pocket helper | handy and upbeat | buoyant bob, waving hand | good assistant | medium; deliberately avoids a yellow pill, goggles, overalls, or other Minions cues |
| Cyclops firefly | magical guide-light | rapid wings, slow float, pulsing lamp | excellent for wayfinding | high, though more fairy-like than software-like |
| Book spirit | narrative and cozy | page flutter, rising wisp | perfect inside Alcove | high as a room-specific companion |
| Tiny curator | knowing and personable | pointer gesture, head nod | most literal museum host | medium-high, but risks becoming a presenter avatar |
| Kinetic familiar | curious, energetic, slightly strange | spring feet, signal ears, comet-tail follow-through | excellent across every room | **highest**; combines pet, idea, and motion system |

## Recommendation: Kinetic familiar

Use **study 06, Kinetic familiar**, as the site-wide guide.

It is the best expression of “I make software move” because movement is not decoration pasted onto it—the silhouette explains movement even in a still frame. Spring legs suggest executable energy; two signal-like antenna ears suggest listening; the comet tail leaves a visible trace; and the single eye preserves continuity with the original restless idea. It is cute without becoming a generic kitten and characterful without leaning on an existing entertainment property.

Suggested production behavior:

1. Let it scamper into the foyer during the first two seconds, notice the visitor, then point its eye and tail toward the first doorway.
2. Keep the idle slow and interruptible. The eye follows the pointer immediately; the body follows with a small delayed lean; the tail settles last.
3. Place it near the next useful interaction, never on top of project copy or media.
4. Use one small speech scrap at a time. Prefer verbs: “look closer”, “turn the page”, “make it move”.
5. Give every room one costume-free material response: reflected green in Alcove, coral warmth near the desktop pet, violet bloom in Keyscape, and monochrome scan lines in the archive.
6. On small screens, reduce it to the eye, antenna ears, and tail beside the room marker. Do not let it consume project-demo space.
7. Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, stop the idle and arrival sequence while retaining direct eye tracking and a static pointing pose.

The **Book spirit** is the strongest secondary option, but it should live specifically in the Alcove room as an exhibit behavior rather than represent the whole portfolio.
